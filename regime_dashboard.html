S&P 500 Regime Monitor

Filtered regime probabilities from a six-state Gaussian HMM on trend momentum, VIX and multi-horizon returns. Calibrated by Baum-Welch (forward-backward) on 1990-2022; everything after is out-of-sample. During US market hours the latest day is a live, partial session.

Method

Observations. Log returns over 1, 5, 21 and 252 trading days; trend momentum as log(close / 200-day SMA); log VIX; log 21-day realised volatility. Standardised with training-window statistics only.

Calibration. Six-state Gaussian HMM with full covariances, fit by Baum-Welch EM (forward-backward) on data through 2022-12-30 with 8 restarts. States are anchored to the regime grid: direction from the sign of the 200-day trend and the 1-month return, volatility from the VIX median. A Gaussian prior on the state means (weight 3000 pseudo-observations) keeps each state on its definition; free EM otherwise drifts "Bear Quiet" into a positive-return dip state because persistent low-VIX bear markets barely exist in S&P history.

Inference. Filtered P(st | y1..t) from the forward pass is what the shading and tiles show. Smoothed and Viterbi paths are computed for comparison. Entropy is the Shannon entropy of the filtered distribution divided by log 6. Tomorrow's regime forecast is filteredt × A.

In-sample log-likelihood is nats per observation. Research tool, not advice.

Constraints and drawbacks

Model assumptions
  • Overlapping returns break conditional independence. The 1-week, 1-month and 1-year returns share most of their daily inputs, so consecutive observations are strongly autocorrelated. The HMM treats them as independent given the state, which inflates persistence and makes filtered probabilities over-confident: entropy sits near zero on most days and only spikes at transitions.
  • Gaussian emissions with fat-tailed data. Daily returns and VIX changes are skewed and heavy-tailed. A crash day is an outlier under every state, so the model can flip abruptly or assign the day to Bear Volatile on magnitude alone.
  • Six states is imposed, not selected. No BIC or cross-validated comparison against 2 to 8 states was run. The 3 x 2 grid is a modelling convention, not something the data asked for.
  • Time-homogeneous transitions. One transition matrix for 1990 to 2026. The persistence of a regime does not depend on how long it has lasted, on macro conditions, or on the calendar.
Calibration and identification
  • Labels are partly imposed. Free EM does not produce a real "Bear Quiet" state; it must be held in place with a prior on the state means. That costs about 0.2 nats per observation of likelihood, and the anchor rule (200-day trend, 1-month return, VIX median) is a hand-chosen definition.
  • Bear Quiet is weakly identified. Only 148 training days satisfy its anchor rule. Its covariance and transitions rest on a small sample, and its strong next-day returns look like dip-buying rather than a stable regime property.
  • Hyperparameters were chosen with hindsight. The prior weight and anchor rule were picked after looking at full-sample outputs, so the out-of-sample split is honest for the parameters but not for those design choices.
  • EM finds local optima. Eight restarts spread over roughly 100 nats of log-likelihood; a different seed set can change state shapes at the margins.
  • Static parameters. Nothing is re-estimated after 2022. Structural changes such as the growth of short-dated options and the shift in VIX behaviour are not learned.
Data and use
  • One index, one volatility gauge. S&P 500 price index (no dividends) and VIX. No breadth, credit spreads, rates, cross-asset or intraday information, and nothing outside US large caps.
  • Filtered calls lag. Switching typically takes 2 to 5 sessions. The shading and probabilities are causal, but smoothed and Viterbi paths use future data and are not tradable.
  • Regime statistics are descriptive. The next-day return table conditions on the same-day filtered label with no costs, slippage or position sizing. It is not a backtest.
  • Warm-up loss. The 252-day return and 200-day average drop the first year of history, and a fresh ticker needs a year of data before the model can run.
  • Data source. Free yfinance downloads with occasional gaps and revisions; the VIX methodology itself changed in 2003 and 2014.
